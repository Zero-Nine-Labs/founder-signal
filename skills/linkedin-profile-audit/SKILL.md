# LinkedIn profile audit

Help any founder make their profile understandable, credible and useful to the audience they want to reach. Deliver an evidence-backed Profile Readiness Score out of 100, prioritised fixes and usable replacement copy. The score is this skill's assessment, not a measured LinkedIn ranking or a promise of leads.

## Establish the founder brief

Use information already supplied. Ask only for missing information that affects the audit:

- Target profile URL, or confirmation that the signed-in user's own profile is the target.
- Primary goal: customers, investors, hiring, credibility, or another stated outcome.
- Intended audience and what the founder or company offers them.

Collect company context, accurate role, real work or expertise, approved proof, desired next step and voice samples where available. Do not require customers, fundraising, testimonials or a large transcript archive. Separate goals if the founder wants more than one. Use their chosen language and locale; otherwise use plain Australian English.

If the primary profile goal is missing, respond with one clarification question asking what the profile should achieve, then stop. Do not include an audit, provisional score, findings or replacement copy in that response. Resume after the founder answers. This stop takes priority over the report and four-copy requirements below. Knowing only the audience and offer does not establish the goal; do not silently assume customer acquisition.

Reuse a goal supplied in the current request or earlier conversation without asking again. Ordinary wording such as attracting relevant businesses to the founder's offer or recruiting engineers is a stated goal; it needs no formal "goal" label or menu selection. A request solely to calculate an already-supplied criterion-status map is an arithmetic check, not a founder audit, and needs no goal clarification.

Keep each founder's identity, brief and evidence separate. Earlier clients' facts and the source guide's examples cannot become this founder's proof. Relevant private context can inform the audit, but confirm permission before including private client names, results or unpublished details in public-facing copy.

## Retrieve and assess

1. Read [profile-access.md](references/profile-access.md) when retrieving a profile. Use the platform's available computer-use tools to inspect LinkedIn in a browser, verify the target identity and record when and how evidence was obtained. If browser access is unavailable, audit user-supplied text, screenshots or a profile export and label that evidence accordingly.
2. Inspect all six sections: headline, About, Featured, banner, Experience and photo. Text retrieval alone cannot establish image quality, cropping, thumbnail readability or mobile layout. Mark those checks unassessed until the relevant pixels or layout are inspected.
3. Read [scoring.md](references/scoring.md) before awarding points. Apply its fixed criteria, anchors, weights, arithmetic and grade boundaries. Save all 20 criterion statuses, generate the score summary and table with the bundled helper or a verified equivalent, and reuse that block verbatim. Cite the observed text, image or supplied fact behind each judgement. Missing retrieval data is not proof that a section is absent.
4. Produce the current-profile report using [report.md](references/report.md), then verify its generated score block against the saved statuses before delivery. Explain the highest-impact gaps first. Full audits get a score and grade; incomplete audits get a provisional range, assessed coverage and outstanding checks. If no executable calculator is available, provide a qualitative audit and mark the numeric score unavailable.

## Draft improvements

Every audit includes a recommended headline, full About section, banner wording and current founder-role Experience description, even when the user says only "audit". This also applies to partial audits when the founder facts support copy. Recommend Featured items using assets that actually exist. If a section is already strong, preserve its effective copy and explain why changing it would add little value.

Separate evidence of what is currently displayed from verified founder facts available for replacement copy. An unseen banner or Experience section stays unassessed in the current score, but it does not prevent drafting those sections from a confirmed role, offer and audience. Use those confirmed facts for all four copy deliverables; ask only for genuinely missing facts rather than asking the founder to reconfirm supplied details.

Write the audience, practical benefit and accurate role clearly. Add specific proof only when supported and approved. Early founders can demonstrate credibility with relevant experience, a working product, a useful resource, documented work or a clear process. Preserve approximate figures and attribution. Missing essential facts remain clearly labelled questions or placeholders; copy containing placeholders is not ready to paste.

For banners, provide the text and a short placement brief. Image generation is separate unless requested. For Featured, explain which item to lead with, its title and why it serves the stated goal. Do not invent links or promise unverified results.

Preserve the founder's vocabulary and level of certainty. Use connected, plain sentences. Remove jargon, generic AI phrasing, forced calls to action, reversal framing and dramatic fragments. Check that every claim and next step is supported by the brief. A customer-acquisition profile might invite an enquiry; an investor or hiring profile needs the appropriate next step instead.

## Re-audit

Retrieve or inspect the updated profile before changing its current score. Replacement drafts remain proposed changes. Compare with a supplied previous audit only when profile identity and rubric version match. If the goal or evidence coverage changed, explain that difference and compare the criteria assessed in both runs. Do not describe extra coverage as a verified improvement.

Return the report and copy in chat unless the user requests an artifact. Do not update LinkedIn, publish, message people, install tools or schedule monitoring through this audit without separate authorisation for that action.
