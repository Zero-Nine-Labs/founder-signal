# Profile Readiness Score

Rubric version: `1.0`. Total available points: **100**.

The section weights and grade boundaries below are the agreed custom rubric. The founder guide informs the checks. FAT Agent informs the audit, prioritisation and recheck pattern. These weights are not empirically validated LinkedIn algorithm factors.

## How to award points

For every criterion record its ID, maximum points, status, awarded points, evidence and short reason.

- **Pass:** full points. The observed section clearly meets the criterion for the stated audience and goal.
- **Partial:** half points. Relevant content exists, but a specific ambiguity or weakness prevents a pass. Identify it.
- **Fail:** zero points. Inspection confirms the criterion is unmet, absent or contradicted.
- **Unassessed:** awarded points are unknown. Necessary evidence or goal context is unavailable. Do not treat this as pass, partial or fail.

Judge each criterion on the current profile. A verified fact in the founder brief can substantiate a displayed claim, but cannot establish that the fact is already displayed. Confirmed section absence fails its checks; an incomplete response, hidden section or failed login leaves them unassessed.

Use the wording in the criterion's own section. A benefit stated in About cannot earn headline H3 points as though it appeared in the headline. Likewise, an About excerpt that explains incoming enquiries but never identifies architecture practices earns partial for A1, even when the audience is stated in the headline or founder brief. H2 rewards a clear offer or area of work; H3 separately rewards what that work does for the reader. A headline that only names the offer and audience, such as "Invoice admin workflows for small accounting firms", implies value but does not state an outcome, so H3 is partial rather than pass. Relevant concrete benefits need not include metrics.

## Criteria

Each criterion is worth **5 points**. Sections differ in the number of criteria. Apply the anchors below; avoid inventing extra deductions or rewarding a preferred writing style.

| ID | Section | Pass | Partial | Fail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Headline | Intended audience or clear context is understandable | Audience/context implied but ambiguous | Generic wording gives neither |
| H2 | Headline | Offer or area of work is concrete and understandable | Category stated with unclear scope or jargon | Offer/work cannot be understood |
| H3 | Headline | Relevant outcome or practical value is clear | Benefit implied or vague | Only titles or buzzwords |
| H4 | Headline | Accurate current role/company relationship | Relevant role given but relationship ambiguous | Role missing, inaccurate or misleading |
| H5 | Headline | A specific, defensible work detail, relevant expertise or approved proof adds support beyond naming the role, offer and audience | Accurate role, offer or audience stated without that additional detail | Only empty enthusiasm/buzzwords, no credible substance, or a contradicted claim |
| A1 | About | Who the founder helps and their relevant problem are clear | Audience or problem clear, but the other is vague | Neither understandable |
| A2 | About | Practical effect of the problem or desired outcome is concrete | Broad benefit/consequence without useful detail | No relevant stakes or outcome |
| A3 | About | Solution, work or approach is explained plainly | Offer named but approach/scope unclear | Reader cannot understand the work |
| A4 | About | Specific work, relevant expertise or approved evidence supports the offer | Relevant credibility stated without enough detail | No supporting substance, or claim contradicted |
| A5 | About | Clear, feasible next step appropriate to the founder's goal | Next step implied, confusing or poorly matched | No next step, or an inspected route is unusable |
| F1 | Featured | At least one accessible, relevant item exists | Item exists but relevance or destination is unclear | Confirmed empty, irrelevant, or all inspected destinations unusable |
| F2 | Featured | Item demonstrates real work, relevant experience, expertise or an outcome | Related material provides weak support | No supporting material, or evidence misleading |
| F3 | Featured | Material gives the intended reader a useful answer or next step | Some utility, but mostly promotion or poorly matched | No useful audience value |
| F4 | Featured | Inspected titles/thumbnails and lead item make the value clear | Material useful but presentation/order obscures it | Inspected presentation unreadable or misleading |
| B1 | Banner | Wording communicates a relevant benefit or offer | Relevant identity/category with a vague benefit | Decorative or generic wording conveys no useful offer/value |
| B2 | Banner | Inspected layouts show legible text and avoid important content being covered or cropped | One inspected layout weak but still usable | Important information unreadable, covered or cropped |
| B3 | Banner | Additional supporting detail beyond the name/main benefit is specific and defensible | Additional supporting detail generic or weak | No additional support, or supporting claim contradicted |
| E1 | Experience | Current role, company and relationship accurate and consistent | Relevant role exists with unclear scope or current status | Current role missing or contradicted |
| E2 | Experience | Plain explanation of company work and founder contribution | One explained, but the other unclear | Neither understandable |
| P1 | Photo | Inspected image clearly shows the founder's face with suitable framing and presentation | Recognisable but a specific framing/quality issue interferes | Confirmed missing or face obscured/unrecognisable |

This yields headline **25**, About **25**, Featured **20**, banner **15**, Experience **10**, photo **5**. Do not transfer points between sections.

Photo recency and whether it still resembles the founder require their confirmation. Note these separately; do not infer age from pixels or add a hidden score deduction. For B2, full points require both desktop and mobile layout evidence. A single layout supports only that layout's assessment; keep B2 unassessed until both are available. F4 requires visual evidence for any thumbnails actually used; text-only extraction cannot verify readability.

An early founder can pass credibility checks using real expertise, a working product, a demonstrable process, a useful resource or documented work. For B3, prototype stage, a concrete example of work or relevant experience can supply the additional support; repeating B1's main benefit does not. Logos, revenue figures, investors, prestigious employers and three Featured items are not compulsory. If a material claim lacks verification, describe it as unverified rather than false. Keep any criterion depending on that claim unassessed unless other inspected content supports its assessment.

## Worked headline judgements

These examples concern H3 and H5 only. Assume the stated work and role are verified; assess the other headline criteria separately.

For H5, an accurate role plus the offer/audience alone gives generic support: partial. A pass needs a specific detail about the work, relevant expertise or approved proof, such as the exception checks below. Do not award full credibility points merely because H1 and H2 pass.

| Criterion | Displayed headline | Status | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| H3 | Founder at Ledger Flow, helping accounting teams stop copying invoice details between systems | Pass | States a concrete practical benefit; no metric is required. |
| H3 | Founder at Ledger Flow, invoice admin workflows for accounting firms | Partial | Names work and audience, but leaves the outcome implied. |
| H3 | Founder, CEO, innovator | Fail | Titles and enthusiasm do not communicate a useful outcome. |
| H5 | Founder at Ledger Flow, building invoice approval workflows with exception checks | Pass | A specific, verified area of work supports credibility, even without client results. |
| H5 | Founder at Ledger Flow, AI consultant | Partial | An accurate role and broad category provide generic support. |
| H5 | Founder at Ledger Flow, invoice admin workflows for accounting firms | Partial | Names the offer and audience without a further detail supporting credibility. |
| H5 | Visionary, disruptor, changing the world | Fail | No defensible work, expertise or proof. |
| H5 | Founder at Ledger Flow, saved clients $1 million | Unassessed | If this is the only credibility support and the figure is unverified, obtain evidence before awarding points. |

The unverified savings example also leaves H3 unassessed: it states an outcome, but that outcome depends on the unverified figure. Do not mark the claim false or say no outcome is stated.

For About A4, a verified description such as "I map how incoming enquiries reach the team and help organise the handoff" can pass as specific work. A client case study, metric or testimonial is not an extra requirement. "I provide consulting" alone is partial because it supplies only a generic category.

If "stop copying invoice details between systems" appears only in About, the second headline still earns partial for H3. Do not borrow evidence from another section or turn a preferred writing style into another deduction.

## Arithmetic and grades

Let `S` be points awarded on assessed criteria. Let `U` be the sum of maximum points for unassessed criteria.

- Coverage: `100 - U` percent, measured by rubric weight.
- Complete (`U = 0`): current score is `S/100`, with a grade.
- Incomplete (`U > 0`): provisional score range is `S` to `S + U` out of 100. No final grade.

Do not rescale assessed criteria to 100, impute unknown values, award default credit, or round before choosing a grade. Display half points where necessary.

| Grade | Complete score |
|---|---|
| A | 90 to 100 |
| B | 80 to below 90 |
| C | 70 to below 80 |
| D | 60 to below 70 |
| F | Below 60 |

Example: 62.5 points earned with 20 unassessed points means **62.5 to 82.5/100 provisional, 80% coverage**. It does not mean 78.1/100 or an inferred grade. If nothing is assessed, say assessment unavailable; 0 to 100 is not a meaningful ranking.

Verify that criterion maxima sum to each section weight and to 100, assessed awards follow the anchors, `0 <= S <= 100 - U`, and `S + U <= 100` before reporting.

Save a JSON object mapping all 20 IDs to `pass`, `partial`, `fail` or `unassessed`. Use [score_profile.py](../scripts/score_profile.py) with Python 3; it requires only the standard library. Its default JSON output provides criterion awards and totals for inspection. It calculates arithmetic and validates inputs; it does not judge evidence or establish that a goal was actually supplied.

Generate the report's score block directly, supplying the founder's already-stated goal:

```sh
python3 <skill-directory>/scripts/score_profile.py --markdown --goal 'Attract client enquiries' < criteria.json > score-block.md
```

Insert the generated block verbatim into the report, retaining its start/end comments. Put evidence and reasons outside the block, keyed by criterion ID. Do not retype current totals elsewhere in the report or accompanying chat. Full section maxima and the reconciled total row remain visible. If a judgement changes, update its status and regenerate the block.

Before delivering either a chat report or an artifact, save the exact report text locally and verify it against the same inputs:

```sh
python3 <skill-directory>/scripts/score_profile.py --verify-report audit.md --goal 'Attract client enquiries' < criteria.json
```

Deliver only after verification succeeds. It rejects missing, duplicated, changed or stale score blocks; it does not validate surrounding prose, evidence judgements or public-facing claims. Use safe quoting or structured execution for the actual goal rather than interpolating user text into shell commands.

Where this helper cannot run, use an executable equivalent that generates and verifies the report block from the same 20 statuses and rubric. Do not install tools just to audit. If no verified calculator is available, deliver the qualitative findings and copy with **numeric score unavailable**. Do not substitute manual arithmetic or a claimed tool result.

## Prioritisation

Prioritise by consequence for the stated goal, then effort. A point loss is not automatically a high-priority issue.

- **High:** materially unclear offer/identity, misleading claim, missing core explanation, or a confirmed broken next step.
- **Medium:** weak proof, unclear benefit, incomplete explanation, or presentation that makes useful material harder to use.
- **Low:** minor wording, ordering or visual polish.

For each finding explain the observed issue, why it matters, the fix and a rough effort estimate. Keep unverified claims separate from proven inaccuracies. Show critical factual issues prominently even when the numerical score is otherwise strong; there are no undisclosed grade caps.
