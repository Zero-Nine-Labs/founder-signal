# LinkedIn content planner

Give any founder a practical content plan tied to their audience, real experience and business goal. Keep topic and funnel stage separate: a topic describes what a post covers; the stage describes what it should do for the reader.

## Establish the brief

Reuse the current founder brief and approved material. Ask only for missing information that changes the plan:

- Founder, company, intended audience, offer and primary goal.
- Requested topic, content pillar, source-document section or funnel-stage filter, including anything to exclude.
- Planning period, local timezone, posting capacity and existing commitments.
- Available notes, conversations, articles, examples, founder opinions and voice samples.

When cadence is unspecified, propose three posts for the next full week, on Monday, Wednesday and Friday. Use the founder's local dates and timezone, defaulting to Australia/Melbourne when unspecified. State this assumption and let the user override it. Suggested dates are planning slots, not scheduled posts or claimed optimal posting times.

Keep founders and companies separate. A previous client's results or examples in the source guide cannot become this founder's evidence. No minimum transcript count or completed profile audit is required.

## Select ideas and plan the week

Read [planning.md](references/planning.md) for buyer-question research, funnel definitions, cadence options and the plan fields.

1. Extract buyer questions from the supplied or accessible source material. Distinguish observed questions, founder-supplied questions and proposed hypotheses. Do not invent customer quotations, recurring patterns or counts.
2. Attach an answer and supporting founder experience, process, example or approved source. Identify what the reader will understand or be able to do. If evidence is thin, propose a supported explanatory angle or list the missing founder input.
3. Apply the requested topic and stage filters together. Do not add other topics or stages simply to satisfy a preferred mix. Select enough distinct ideas for the agreed cadence, prioritising audience relevance, useful substance and available support.
4. Give each post one main point, one purpose and a suitable format. Different posts about one topic need different questions, examples or decisions, not repeated wording.
5. Map the ideas to the requested local dates. Check the actual weekdays, post count and source availability. Explain any gap that prevents a complete plan instead of filling it with invented proof.

Before delivering, compare each post's one-sentence answer and reader takeaway. Different hooks, questions or formats do not make posts distinct when their bodies would teach the same thing from the same example. Merge that overlap and find a genuinely different supported answer. If the source cannot support enough distinct posts, keep the requested calendar slots, mark the gaps source needed and ask for the specific extra input. Do not label a repeated angle ready to draft merely to fill the calendar.

## Deliver and hand off

Return a concise calendar using the fields in the reference, plus the source/proof and missing inputs for each post. Mark supported briefs ready to draft and incomplete briefs source needed. Mark a post drafted only when a draft actually exists.

Include a short preparation rhythm for gathering inputs, drafting and reviewing the week's posts. These are proposed tasks, not reminders or booked meetings. Keep full post copy out of a planning-only request.

Each selected brief must be usable by a writer without reconstructing the strategy. Include the founder/audience context, topic, buyer question, stage, main point, purpose, source facts, proof, format, voice and any required next step. If the post-writing skill is available, pass the brief to it when the user requests drafting; otherwise the brief stands alone.

Use supplied performance and feedback to inform the next plan. Judge visibility, trust and buying intent against each post's purpose. Keep missing metrics unknown and separate observed outcomes from uncertain attribution. Do not rebuild the strategy around one post or treat impressions as leads.

Return the plan in chat unless the user requests an artifact. Planning does not authorise publishing, LinkedIn edits, messages, calendar writes, recurring automation or tool installation. For optional live LinkedIn inspection, use the environment's approved computer-use workflow and label the evidence source.
