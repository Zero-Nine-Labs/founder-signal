# Buyer questions, topics and weekly plans

## Start from useful questions

Use the material already available: calls, FAQs, objections, support notes, product feedback, documented work, founder interviews or existing writing. Keep the buyer's actual language when supplied, with its source. Label a question proposed when it is inferred from the audience rather than observed.

An idea needs a question the audience cares about and something credible the founder can contribute. A real process, useful explanation or supported point of view can be sufficient. A customer result is not compulsory.

For each candidate record the question, its basis, the relevant topic/pillar, the possible answer, supporting source and any missing information. Ask focused founder questions when personal experience is essential. Do not request a large interview or invent a story to fill a weekly slot.

## Topic and funnel stage are independent

A content pillar is a repeatable subject area. It can contain several subtopics and buyer questions. One pillar can produce posts at different stages.

| Stage | Reader's situation and post purpose | Suitable material |
|---|---|---|
| TOF: awareness | New reader discovers the founder or recognises a relevant problem | Supported founder stories, company-building lessons, personal observations with a useful takeaway, relevant industry commentary |
| MOF: consideration | Interested reader understands an approach, evaluates a trade-off or builds trust in the founder's thinking | Processes, frameworks, explanations, decisions, worked examples, supported opinions |
| BOF: decision | Buyer understands the offer, resolves a purchase question or examines evidence | Accurate offer FAQs, objections, product explanations, approved case studies or testimonials |

Classify by reader intent and the job of the post, not whether it mentions a product. A technical explanation can be MOF; a buying FAQ can be BOF without a testimonial. TOF need not chase unrelated reach. Use plain stage names in conversation when abbreviations would distract.

When a user selects a topic, funnel stage or document section, respect that boundary. A week of only consideration posts is valid. Do not replace the user's constraint with a compulsory funnel mix.

## Cadence and mix

The guide proposes three to five posts per week. Use a sustainable capacity rather than promising a performance benefit from volume.

- Three posts: one awareness, one consideration and one decision post can be a useful starting mix when supported material exists.
- Five posts: the guide suggests two awareness, two consideration and one decision post, or one awareness, three consideration and one decision post.

These are options. Choose from the goal, evidence, audience feedback and explicit filters. Without decision-stage proof, an accurate offer FAQ may work. Otherwise use another supported idea and explain the gap. Do not fabricate a customer case to satisfy the mix.

For longer requested periods, use actual calendar dates and the agreed weekly capacity. The guide's monthly quantity is a planning target, not a reason to invent or discard calendar slots. It supplies no fixed 90-day growth milestones.

## Calendar and writer brief

Use a compact table with post ID, proposed date/day, stage, topic/pillar, buyer question and angle, purpose, format and status. Preserve these facts even if combining columns improves readability. Describe sources and missing inputs beneath the table by post ID when they would make it too wide.

For each post, make this brief available:

- Founder/company, audience, offer and business goal.
- Post ID and proposed date.
- Topic/pillar, selected source section if any, and exclusions.
- Buyer question and whether it was observed, supplied or proposed.
- Funnel stage, one main point and a concrete purpose.
- Source material, approved facts and the founder's relevant example or point of view.
- Proposed format/media, voice requirements and any appropriate next step.
- Missing facts, proof, permissions or assets, with readiness status.

A plain source reference or short fact list is sufficient. Do not paste private transcripts or customer details into public-facing text. Internal material can inform a brief without being approved for publication.

## Review the week

Record feedback relevant to the post's purpose: audience relevance, useful discussion, profile interest, enquiries or other business signals when supplied. Treat one week as preliminary evidence. Look for repeated patterns over a longer period before changing the content strategy.

Account-level profile visits or sales activity are not automatically attributable to one post. If outcome data is unavailable, review the process and source gaps instead of inventing a performance report.
